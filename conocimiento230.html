Conocimiento 230

A resource-management platform that already existed and still wasn't working. The fix wasn't a redesign — it was figuring out what the first version got wrong.

Duration

About

Conocimiento 230 manages organizational resources and doubles as a repository of information — a records manager for the organization's own experience. It looked like a reasonable product on paper. It wasn't working in practice.

My contribution

Lead Product Designer, which here meant owning the uncomfortable part first: figuring out why a shipped product wasn't landing, before touching any screens.

  • Led understanding of the need and definition of scope; prepared roadmap and release planning
  • Defined business rules and development stages; built a work plan with Scrum tools
  • Produced wireframes, user stories, personas, and flows; ran UX research for data collection
  • Prototyped, gathered feedback, and iterated on validation of proposals
  • Ran usability and heuristic testing; led QA and stakeholder review
  • Launched the first version and planned next steps

What was actually wrong

The previous version wasn't broken in any obvious way. It just didn't solve the user's real need — it solved an adjacent, easier-to-build version of that need. Research made that distinction clear fast, which was both good and bad news: good because it meant the fix was findable, bad because it meant a rebuild, not a patch.

Process

  1. 1

    Definition

    The scope and need were defined in collaboration with stakeholders to define the feasibility of the restructuring or redesign.

  2. 2

    Ideation

    The site map, information architecture were defined, and wireframes and user stories were developed.

  3. 3

    Prototype

    Functional prototypes were presented to evaluate ideas and pass them on to development.

  4. 4

    Validation

    The first version of the application was released and feedback was collected.

Results & impact

The rebuild, grounded in what research actually found rather than what the original spec assumed, produced a tool that handles more scenarios and serves a wider range of user profiles than its predecessor.

  • Confirmed the earlier version solved the wrong problem, not just the problem poorly
  • Rebuild around research insights let more scenarios and profiles be served

Business goals

The redesign realigned the product with what the organization actually needed from a resource-management and knowledge-repository tool — not what the first version assumed they needed. That gave the team a validated foundation to plan future releases from, instead of guessing again.
